MODEL RELEASE · STONEFISH V5

Introducing Stonefish v5

Stonefish v5 rewrites the decision system around one idea: every legal move gets a complete score before Stonefish decides.

Earlier Stonefish models became stronger by adding new layers: tactical calculation, positional judgment, opening knowledge, mating patterns, and opponent restriction. Stonefish v5 does something more fundamental. It changes how all of those ideas interact.

Two upgrades define the release: a unified points architecture, and an opening book that has become advice instead of law.

Change one: every move gets points

Stonefish v4 and v4.5 used staged decisions. One layer could narrow the candidate set, then the next layer judged only the survivors. That made each priority clear, but it also meant a move eliminated early could never recover because of strengths elsewhere.

Stonefish v5 removes that bottleneck. Every legal root move stays eligible. Every one receives tactical, positional, strategic, and knowledge points. The move with the strongest total score wins.

Stonefish no longer asks which move survives the hierarchy. It asks which move has the strongest complete case.

Tactical safety for every legal move

v5 keeps three-ply tactical reasoning — our move, the opponent’s reply, our response — but applies it to every legal root move instead of using an older model’s candidate gate.

Mate receives overwhelming priority. Immediate tactical loss is punished heavily. Captures, promotions, checking moves, the opponent’s strongest response, and Stonefish’s best recovery all feed into the tactical score.

The difference is that tactics now contributes to a total rather than prematurely deciding which moves the rest of the engine is allowed to see.

A unified positional scorecard

Stonefish v5 scores material, mobility, development, center control, minor-piece centralization, king protection, king freedom, pawn structure, rook activity, king placement, board control, hanging pieces, bishop-pair value, and passed pawns.

That gives the engine something the older ordered tie-break chain could not express naturally: trade-offs. A move can give up a little mobility for a much safer king. A passed pawn can outweigh a quieter positional preference. A tactical opportunity can justify an imperfect structure.

Everything speaks the same language: points.

Passed pawns and conversion pressure

v5 sharpens endgame judgment by tracking passed-pawn progress and danger. It considers how close a passer is to promotion, whether its route is blocked or controlled, whether major pieces or kings stand in front of it, whether the pawn is attacked, and whether the promotion square can be covered.

It also applies enormous pressure against repetition, especially when Stonefish is ahead, and can value moves that reset the fifty-move clock when necessary.

The engine is not only trying to become better. It is trying to convert better.

Change two: the opening book becomes advice

Stonefish v4.5 introduced the weighted 50-line opening repertoire and adaptive compatible-line fallback. v5 keeps that preparation, but changes what a book move means.

In v4.5, when a valid book move existed and no tactical pattern overrode it, theory could directly control the move.

In v5, the book move receives a strong point bonus.

That sounds like a small change. It is not.

The book can win the argument. It no longer automatically wins the vote.

Sometimes Stonefish follows theory. Sometimes it says it knows better.

If the repertoire recommends a strong main-line move and the rest of the position agrees, the book bonus helps that move rise to the top. Stonefish follows its preparation.

But every alternative move is being scored at the same time. If another move is tactically superior, avoids a serious threat, creates a much stronger passed pawn, improves the complete position enough, or otherwise earns a higher total, v5 can reject the book move.

Stonefish is no longer asking only, “Am I still in theory?”

It asks, “What does theory recommend — and is that still the best move on this board?”

The opening system is flexible in two different ways

The adaptive repertoire behavior from v4.5 remains. If the opponent deviates, Stonefish can continue with another opening line that still matches the real move history.

v5 then adds another layer of flexibility: even when a prepared continuation remains available, Stonefish’s unified evaluation can decide not to play it.

That gives v5 both repertoire flexibility — adapting the book to the actual game — and evaluation flexibility — overriding the book when Stonefish’s own scoring system finds something better.

Old Stonefish knowledge becomes a vote too

v5 retains a heritage signal from the strongest pre-v5 decision pattern. The move that the older tactical-positional process would have preferred gets a large bonus.

But even that is no longer absolute. Heritage can influence the decision without deleting the alternatives.

That is the philosophy of v5: proven knowledge deserves weight, not unquestioned authority.

Why the points architecture matters

Stonefish v5 does not achieve this jump by simply adding another search ply. The major change is simultaneous evaluation.

Tactics, material, activity, structure, king safety, passed-pawn danger, preparation, mating geometry, opponent restriction, repetition pressure, and inherited Stonefish knowledge can all influence the same move score.

No small early preference has to erase a move that is overwhelmingly better in the complete position. No opening line gets to silence the tactical engine. No legacy rule gets to stop the positional system from making its case.

For the first time, Stonefish can hear every part of itself before choosing.

MODEL RELEASE · STONEFISH V4.5

Introducing Stonefish v4.5

Stonefish v4.5 is the moment the Stonefish revolution gains something new: knowledge before calculation.

Stonefish v4 already combined the three-ply tactical core with a broad positional decision layer. Stonefish v4.5 keeps all of that and adds three major knowledge systems: a weighted opening repertoire, named mating-pattern recognition, and inverse positional logic that evaluates how effectively a move restricts the opponent.

A 50-line opening repertoire

Stonefish v4.5 includes a weighted 50-line opening repertoire: 25 lines for White and 25 for Black, centered on high-theory, engine-friendly main lines.

If the opponent leaves the selected branch, v4.5 discards it and re-weights only the repertoire lines still compatible with the actual game.

Named mating-pattern recognition

The engine recognizes ladder, triangle, back-rank, smothered, Arabian, queen-and-rook kill-box, and Boden-style mating geometry.

Why does v3 fare better against v4.5 than v4 does?

Stonefish v4 is stronger than v3 head-to-head, but strength is not perfectly transitive. v4.5 is especially well-equipped to suppress the exact positional variables v4 tries to optimize: mobility, king freedom, center control, and board control.

v3 is simpler and more tactically driven, so some of that targeted positional restriction has less leverage against its style.

v4 is stronger against v3. But v3 is the less convenient opponent for v4.5.
