MODEL RELEASE · STONEFISH V3

Introducing Stonefish v3

Stonefish v3 marks the next major leap in the Stonefish revolution: from immediate tactical awareness to genuine multi-step calculation.

Stonefish v2 learned to recognize mate in one, avoid immediate mate, and reduce the opponent’s best next capture. Stonefish v3 goes much further. It now evaluates a three-ply sequence: our move, the opponent’s reply, and our response.

For the first time, Stonefish is not only asking what happens next. It is asking what happens after that.

Three-ply material intelligence

At the heart of Stonefish v3 is a new worst-case material engine.

For every candidate move, Stonefish examines the opponent’s possible replies. For each of those replies, it then looks at its own best response. The sequence is scored from the material Stonefish gains immediately, minus the material the opponent can win back, plus the material Stonefish can recover on the third ply.

Then Stonefish chooses the move with the strongest worst-case result.

This is a profound change. Stonefish v2 could avoid obvious one-move disasters. Stonefish v3 can recognize that a move which looks dangerous immediately may still be strong if it forces a favorable continuation — and that a move which looks safe at first glance may collapse one move later.

It plans through the opponent

The defining idea in v3 is simple: the opponent gets a vote.

Stonefish no longer evaluates its move in isolation. It assumes the opponent will answer as strongly as possible, then asks whether Stonefish still comes out ahead after its own best response.

That makes v3 materially more resilient. It is harder to bait, harder to trick with one-move threats, and far better at distinguishing real gains from temporary ones.

Instead of asking “Can I take this piece?”, Stonefish v3 asks “What can they do back — and what can I do after that?”

Mate still comes first

Stonefish v3 preserves the most important tactical priorities introduced in v2.

If checkmate in one is available, it takes it. If a candidate move allows the opponent to mate in one, Stonefish avoids that move whenever a safe alternative exists.

But v3 adds another layer: mating opportunities on its own third ply are valued above every material outcome.

Material matters. Checkmate matters more.

More precise piece values

Stonefish v3 also introduces a more refined material model. Bishops are valued at 3.1 rather than being treated exactly the same as knights.

That difference is small by design, but it gives the engine a more precise way to compare exchanges when otherwise similar lines compete.

The important change is not the decimal itself. It is that Stonefish now has enough depth for those distinctions to matter across a sequence of moves.

Why v3 is so much stronger

It looks beyond the immediate reply. v2 could see danger one move ahead. v3 can evaluate whether the position remains favorable after Stonefish answers back.

It understands recovery. A temporary material loss is no longer automatically bad if Stonefish can win more material back on the third ply.

It sees hidden danger. Moves that appear safe after one reply can be rejected when the next layer reveals a losing sequence.

It assumes resistance. Stonefish v3 optimizes the worst-case continuation rather than hoping the opponent chooses a weak reply.

It preserves tactical priorities. Immediate mate and mate avoidance still override ordinary material considerations.

These changes reinforce each other. v3 is not stronger because it has one new trick. It is stronger because its decision process now evaluates a sequence rather than a snapshot.

From awareness to calculation

Stonefish v1 was unpredictable.

Stonefish v2 became aware.

Stonefish v3 calculates.

The Stonefish revolution is no longer built only on possibility or immediate danger. It now reasons across sequences, anticipates resistance, and chooses moves that remain strong after the opponent fights back.

Stonefish is no longer just reacting to the board. It is starting to look through it.

MODEL RELEASE · STONEFISH V2

Introducing Stonefish v2

Stonefish v2 is the next revolutionary step in the Stonefish project: the speed and decisiveness of the original idea, now with tactical awareness.

Stonefish v1 proved that a chess engine could reject predictability and operate directly from the full legal move space. Stonefish v2 takes that revolution forward by asking a harder question before it moves: what happens immediately after this move?

That single change transforms the character of the engine.

From possibility to consequence

Stonefish v2 no longer treats every legal move as equal. It evaluates immediate tactical consequences with a strict priority system.

First, if Stonefish has checkmate in one, it takes it. Second, it checks whether a candidate move would allow the opponent to deliver checkmate in one. If safer alternatives exist, those losing moves are rejected. Third, among the remaining candidates, Stonefish looks one reply ahead and asks how much material the opponent could take immediately.

This creates a compact but meaningful defensive intelligence layer. Stonefish v2 is no longer simply moving through the possibility space. It is filtering that space through survival, punishment, and risk.

Mate awareness changes everything

The most important breakthrough in Stonefish v2 is simple: it understands immediate checkmate.

If the winning move is available, Stonefish does not wander past it. It ends the game. Just as importantly, it no longer casually walks into a one-move checkmate when a safe alternative exists.

That creates a sharp distinction between legal and viable. Stonefish v1 knew what could be played. Stonefish v2 begins to understand what must not be played.

Defensive foresight

Avoiding mate is only the first layer. When no immediate mate is involved, Stonefish v2 measures the strongest capture available to the opponent after each candidate move.

Piece values give the engine a simple risk model: pawns matter, minor pieces matter more, rooks matter more again, and queens are too valuable to expose casually. Stonefish then minimizes the opponent’s best immediate gain.

The result is not heavyweight search. It is something leaner: immediate tactical discipline.

Why v2 wins

It converts immediate wins. If checkmate is available in one move, Stonefish v2 recognizes it and plays it.

It refuses immediate tactical defeat. Moves that allow mate in one are filtered out whenever safer options exist.

It reduces free material losses. By examining the opponent’s strongest immediate capture, Stonefish v2 actively prefers positions where less material is exposed.

These mechanisms reinforce each other. A position is no longer judged only by whether a move is legal. Stonefish v2 now asks whether a move wins immediately, loses immediately, or gives the opponent an immediate material opportunity.

That is the beginning of tactical judgment.

Still fast. Far more dangerous.

Stonefish v2 remains deliberately focused. It does not attempt to calculate an enormous game tree or bury every move under layers of evaluation.

Instead, it concentrates on the most urgent facts on the board: checkmate, survival, and immediate material danger.

That focus gives Stonefish v2 a powerful combination of responsiveness and discipline. It retains the directness of Stonefish while eliminating entire classes of catastrophic decisions.

The Stonefish revolution moves forward

Stonefish v1 opened the door by proving that a chess engine could be built around a radically different decision process.

Stonefish v2 steps through that door.

It can finish games when the opportunity appears. It can recognize immediate disaster before committing to it. It can protect valuable material by looking at the opponent’s next move.

And in 1,000 direct games against Stonefish v1, it did not lose once.

Stonefish is no longer only unpredictable. Now it is aware.

MODEL RELEASE · STONEFISH V1

Introducing Stonefish v1

Stonefish v1 is a new kind of chess engine built around a simple idea: stop being predictable.

Traditional chess programs are obsessed with finding the move. Stonefish v1 takes a radically different approach. It looks at the full set of legal possibilities in a position and makes its choice from that entire move space.

The result is an engine that refuses to be boxed in by conventional expectations.

Every legal move is part of the plan

At the heart of Stonefish v1 is a clean, uncompromising decision process: read the position, generate every legal move, select from the complete legal move set, and play immediately.

Stonefish v1 does not spend its time imitating standard lines or repeatedly converging on the same familiar continuation. Every legal option remains alive until the moment a move is chosen.

It’s lightweight. It’s fast. And most importantly, it’s unpredictable.

Unpredictability as a feature

Chess is often treated purely as a search problem. But against a human opponent, predictability is information. Stonefish v1 attacks that assumption directly.

Because its decisions are drawn from the legal move space rather than a fixed preferred line, preparing for it is fundamentally different. You cannot simply memorize what Stonefish “likes” to play and expect the same position to unfold the same way twice.

One game might begin quietly. Another might take an immediate turn into something completely unexpected. Stonefish v1 embraces the enormous possibility space of chess instead of compressing it into one approved answer.

Built to move

Stonefish v1 is intentionally compact. There is no heavyweight evaluation stack standing between the position and the move. Once legal moves are available, Stonefish can make a decision with minimal overhead.

That gives v1 a straightforward strength: responsiveness. Stonefish stays focused on the essential job — identify what can legally be played and make a move.

A different kind of chess engine

Stonefish v1 is not trying to play chess the way every other engine does. It is an experiment in freedom, variation, and machine unpredictability.

What happens when every legal move gets a chance?

That experiment begins with Stonefish v1.
