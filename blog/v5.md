# Introducing Stonefish v5

Stonefish v5 is the biggest decision-system rewrite in the Stonefish revolution so far.

The headline result is impossible to ignore: **2,000 wins, 0 losses, 0 draws** across 2,000 direct games against Stonefish v4 and Stonefish v4.5.

But the important story is not simply that v5 wins. It is *why*.

Stonefish v5 makes two fundamental changes:

1. it replaces the old staged decision chain with a **unified points system** that scores every legal move at once,
2. it transforms the opening book from an instruction into **weighted advice** that Stonefish can accept or overrule.

Together, those changes turn Stonefish from an engine that follows a hierarchy into one that compares complete alternatives.

## Change one: everything becomes points

Earlier Stonefish models made decisions in stages. A tactical layer could eliminate moves first. Positional tie-breakers would only see the survivors. Opening knowledge could take control when a book line matched. The architecture was powerful, but it meant a move rejected early could never recover later because of strengths elsewhere.

Stonefish v5 changes that completely.

**Every legal root move is scored.** No move has to survive a long chain of hard filters before the rest of the engine is allowed to judge it.

Each move receives a combined score built from tactical safety, material, positional quality, opening knowledge, mating geometry, opponent restriction, conversion pressure, and other strategic signals.

The move with the highest total wins.

That sounds simple, but it changes the meaning of every subsystem Stonefish has developed.

A move can now be slightly worse in one category and still be clearly best overall. A move with exceptional tactical value can overwhelm a modest positional disadvantage. A positional move can beat a book move if the board says the position demands it. A familiar legacy choice can receive credit without automatically eliminating every alternative.

Stonefish no longer asks a sequence of yes-or-no questions.

It asks: **what is this move worth in total?**

## Tactical judgment for every legal move

The tactical component is still rooted in the three-ply idea that transformed Stonefish earlier: our move, the opponent's reply, and our response.

The difference is that v5 runs that tactical scoring across **every legal root move**, rather than using an earlier model's winner set as a gate.

Immediate checkmate receives overwhelming priority. Moves that allow immediate mate are punished overwhelmingly. Captures, promotions, checking moves, the opponent's strongest reply, and Stonefish's best immediate recovery all contribute to a tactical floor.

This preserves tactical discipline without allowing the tactical subsystem to prematurely erase strategically superior alternatives.

## A real weighted position score

Stonefish v5 also converts positional ideas into simultaneous values rather than ordered tie-breakers.

Its position score can account for material balance, mobility, development, center control, minor-piece centralization, king protection, king freedom, pawn structure, rook activity, king placement, board control, hanging pieces, the bishop pair, and passed pawns.

These are not separate doors a move must pass through one by one. They all contribute to the same comparison.

That distinction matters. Chess positions are full of trade-offs. More mobility can be worth accepting a small structural imperfection. A passed pawn can outweigh a quieter positional preference. King safety can matter more than centralization in one position and less in another.

The points architecture finally gives Stonefish a natural way to express those trade-offs.

## Passed pawns and conversion pressure

v5 also gives passed pawns much sharper treatment. It tracks how advanced they are, whether their next square is blocked or controlled, whether major pieces or kings stand in front of them, whether the pawn itself is attacked, and whether the promotion square can be controlled after an advance.

Danger rises rapidly as a passer approaches promotion, and enemy passed pawns are treated particularly seriously.

Repetition is also punished aggressively, especially when Stonefish is ahead. The engine can reward moves that reset the fifty-move clock when necessary and heavily discourage drifting into repeated positions that throw away conversion chances.

The result is an engine that does not merely obtain advantages. It is increasingly designed to finish them.

## Change two: the opening book becomes advice, not law

Stonefish v4.5 introduced the 50-line weighted opening repertoire and adaptive compatible-line fallback. That was a major leap: Stonefish could finally begin games with prepared theory instead of reconstructing every opening from general principles.

Stonefish v5 keeps that knowledge — but changes its authority.

In v4.5, once the engine had a valid book move and no overriding mating pattern, the repertoire could directly determine the move.

In v5, a book move earns **points**.

That means Stonefish knows what theory recommends, values that recommendation strongly, and still asks whether another legal move is better according to the entire position.

This is the crucial difference.

**The book can win the argument. It no longer automatically wins the vote.**

## Stonefish can disagree with its own preparation

Suppose the opening repertoire recommends a known main-line move. v5 gives that move a substantial bonus because prepared theory is valuable.

But every competing legal move is still receiving its own tactical and positional score.

If the book move is safe, coherent, and strong, the bonus helps it rise to the top and Stonefish follows theory.

If another move produces a clearly superior tactical result, avoids a major threat, creates a dangerous passed pawn, improves the total position enough, or otherwise scores higher, Stonefish can reject its own book recommendation.

That is a fundamentally more mature use of opening knowledge.

Stonefish v5 does not ask, “Am I still in the book?” and stop thinking.

It asks, **“What does the book recommend, and is that still the best move here?”**

## The opening book is still adaptive

The underlying repertoire intelligence remains valuable. Stonefish still has the weighted White and Black opening families introduced in v4.5, and the opening system still tracks which lines remain compatible with the real move history.

If the opponent deviates, the repertoire can adapt to compatible branches rather than treating one broken sequence as the end of all preparation.

But v5 adds a second level of flexibility on top of that branch adaptation: even when a valid prepared move exists, the unified score can decide that Stonefish's own evaluation has a better idea.

So there are now two kinds of intelligence in the opening:

- **repertoire flexibility** — adapt the prepared line to what was actually played,
- **evaluation flexibility** — override prepared theory when the full points system prefers something else.

Preparation and independent judgment finally coexist.

## Heritage becomes a vote too

v5 even keeps a signal from the strongest pre-v5 decision pattern. The older tactical/positional process can nominate the move it would have preferred, and that move receives a weighted heritage bonus.

But just like the opening book, heritage is not absolute.

It is one vote among many.

That is the philosophy of v5 in one sentence: **knowledge matters, but nothing gets to stop the evaluation early.**

## The test: 2,000 direct games

For the v5 benchmark, only games involving Stonefish v5 count: 1,000 against Stonefish v4 and 1,000 against Stonefish v4.5.

The result was perfect.

**2,000 wins · 0 losses · 0 draws**

That is a **100.0% win rate** across 2,000 direct games.

### v5 vs v4: 1000–0

Across 1,000 games:

- Stonefish v5: **1,000 wins, 0 losses, 0 draws**
- Stonefish v4: **0 wins, 1,000 losses, 0 draws**
- Stonefish v5 win rate: **100.0%**

Every game ended in a Stonefish v5 win.

### v5 vs v4.5: 1000–0

Across another 1,000 games:

- Stonefish v5: **1,000 wins, 0 losses, 0 draws**
- Stonefish v4.5: **0 wins, 1,000 losses, 0 draws**
- Stonefish v5 win rate: **100.0%**

Again: every game ended in a Stonefish v5 win.

That second result is especially important because v4.5 already carried the opening repertoire, mating-pattern knowledge, opponent restriction, and the v4 positional foundation. v5 does not win by throwing those ideas away. It wins by changing how they are combined.

## Why the points system is such a large jump

The older architecture was lexicographic: answer one question, reduce the candidate set, then ask the next question.

v5 is comparative: score the whole move.

That allows tactical and positional information to interact rather than merely take turns. It prevents a small early preference from permanently hiding a move that is much stronger overall. It also means knowledge systems can influence Stonefish without becoming rigid commands.

Most importantly, v5 can judge trade-offs.

That is what the point system really introduces. Not just more heuristics — a common language between them.

Material, safety, activity, structure, preparation, mating geometry, restriction, conversion, passed-pawn danger, and legacy knowledge can finally compete on the same scoreboard.

## From following knowledge to judging it

Stonefish v1 was unpredictable.

Stonefish v2 became aware.

Stonefish v3 learned to calculate.

Stonefish v4 learned to build.

Stonefish v4.5 arrived prepared.

**Stonefish v5 learns to judge.**

It can value preparation without obeying it blindly. It can preserve proven Stonefish behavior without being trapped by it. It can compare every legal move using one unified points architecture and choose the move whose complete case is strongest.

The opening book still speaks.

The tactical engine still speaks.

The positional engine still speaks.

But for the first time, **Stonefish hears all of them before it decides.**
