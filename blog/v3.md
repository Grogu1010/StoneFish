# Introducing Stonefish v3

Stonefish v3 marks the next major leap in the Stonefish revolution: from immediate tactical awareness to genuine multi-step calculation.

Stonefish v2 learned to recognize mate in one, avoid immediate mate, and reduce the opponent’s best next capture. Stonefish v3 goes much further. It now evaluates a three-ply sequence: our move, the opponent’s reply, and our response.

For the first time, Stonefish is not only asking what happens next. It is asking what happens after that.

## Three-ply material intelligence

At the heart of Stonefish v3 is a new worst-case material engine.

For every candidate move, Stonefish examines the opponent’s possible replies. For each of those replies, it then looks at its own best response. It scores the sequence as:

- material gained immediately,
- minus material the opponent can win in reply,
- plus material Stonefish can win back on the third ply.

Then it chooses the move with the strongest worst-case result.

This is a profound change. Stonefish v2 could avoid obvious one-move disasters. Stonefish v3 can now recognize that a move which looks dangerous immediately may still be strong if it forces a favorable continuation — and that a move which looks safe at first glance may collapse one move later.

## It plans through the opponent

The defining idea in v3 is simple: the opponent gets a vote.

Stonefish no longer evaluates its move in isolation. It assumes the opponent will answer as strongly as possible, then asks whether Stonefish still comes out ahead after its own best response.

That makes v3 materially more resilient. It is harder to bait, harder to trick with one-move threats, and far better at distinguishing real gains from temporary ones.

Instead of asking, “Can I take this piece?” Stonefish v3 asks, “If I take this piece, what is the strongest thing they can do back — and what can I do after that?”

That is the beginning of real calculation.

## Mate still comes first

Stonefish v3 preserves the most important tactical priorities introduced in v2.

If checkmate in one is available, it takes it. If a candidate move allows the opponent to mate in one, Stonefish avoids that move whenever a safe alternative exists.

But v3 adds another layer: mating opportunities on its own third ply are valued above every material outcome.

Material matters. Checkmate matters more.

## More precise piece values

Stonefish v3 also introduces a more refined material model. Bishops are valued at 3.1 rather than being treated exactly the same as knights.

That difference is small by design, but it gives the engine a more precise way to compare exchanges when otherwise similar lines compete.

The important change is not the decimal itself. It is that Stonefish now has enough depth for those distinctions to matter across a sequence of moves.

## The test: 2,000 direct games

For this release, the only benchmarks that matter are Stonefish v3’s direct matchups against the two Stonefish generations that came before it: 1,000 games against v1 and 1,000 games against v2.

Across those 2,000 games, Stonefish v3 produced:

**1,940 wins · 5 losses · 55 draws**

That is a **97.0% win rate** and a **0.3% loss rate**.

### Stonefish v3 vs Stonefish v2

Across 1,000 games:

- Stonefish v3: **948 wins, 5 losses, 47 draws**
- Stonefish v2: **5 wins, 948 losses, 47 draws**
- Stonefish v3 win rate: **94.8%**

This is the clearest measure of the leap from immediate tactical awareness to three-ply calculation. Stonefish v2 was already capable of avoiding mate-in-one and minimizing immediate material exposure. Stonefish v3 still beat it in 948 of 1,000 games.

### Stonefish v3 vs Stonefish v1

Across 1,000 games:

- Stonefish v3: **992 wins, 0 losses, 8 draws**
- Stonefish v1: **0 wins, 992 losses, 8 draws**
- Stonefish v3 win rate: **99.2%**

Stonefish v3 did not lose a single game to v1.

## Why v3 is so much stronger

Stonefish v2 transformed the engine by teaching it immediate consequence. Stonefish v3 transforms it again by teaching it continuation.

It is stronger because it no longer stops at the opponent’s reply. It can see whether material comes back. It can tell the difference between a real loss and a temporary sacrifice. It can reject moves whose danger only becomes visible one move later. It can select moves that remain strong even against the opponent’s best answer.

Most importantly, it optimizes for the worst case.

That means a move is not considered good because one favorable reply exists. It must survive the strongest reply Stonefish can find.

## From awareness to calculation

Stonefish v1 was unpredictable.

Stonefish v2 became aware.

Stonefish v3 calculates.

The Stonefish revolution is no longer built only on possibility or immediate danger. It now reasons across sequences, anticipates resistance, and chooses moves that remain strong after the opponent fights back.

**Stonefish is no longer just reacting to the board. It is starting to look through it.**
