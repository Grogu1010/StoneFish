# Introducing Stonefish v4

Stonefish v4 is the next major step in the Stonefish revolution: the tactical calculation of v3, now paired with a full positional decision layer.

Stonefish v3 learned to calculate through a three-ply sequence and optimize the worst-case material outcome. Stonefish v4 keeps that foundation intact. It does not throw away v3’s tactical discipline and it does not simply search one move deeper.

Instead, v4 asks a new question after v3 has already found the tactically best candidates:

**When several moves are materially equal, which one actually gives Stonefish the better position?**

That is where Stonefish v4 begins.

## Tactical strength first. Positional intelligence second.

Stonefish v4 is built directly on top of the v3 engine.

The process starts exactly where v3 starts: mate safety, three-ply material calculation, and worst-case evaluation decide which moves survive. Only moves tied by that tactical engine reach the new v4 decision layer.

That matters because v4 does not trade tactical strength for positional style. Position is used to separate moves that v3 already considers equally strong.

Stonefish v4 therefore combines two different kinds of intelligence:

- tactical calculation to eliminate losing or inferior material lines,
- positional judgment to choose the strongest continuation among the survivors.

It is the first Stonefish model designed to care not only about what a move wins, but about what kind of position it leaves behind.

## Development becomes a decision

In the opening, v4 actively prefers developing minor pieces when the tactical result is tied.

Knights and bishops are rewarded for leaving their home squares. Early queen movement is treated with discipline while development is still underway. Castling is preferred immediately when the tactical layer says it is safe and materially equal.

This gives Stonefish something previous versions did not have: a sense of how to build a position.

The engine is no longer waiting for tactics to appear before making progress. It can improve its pieces, secure its king, and prepare the board for what comes next.

## It stops leaving valuable pieces hanging

Stonefish v4 explicitly checks whether its own pieces are attacked and undefended.

Among tactically equal moves, it prefers positions that do not leave high-value material exposed for free.

That sounds simple, but it changes the quality of play dramatically. A move can survive a short material search and still create a strategically fragile position. v4 adds a second line of defense by asking whether an important piece has simply been left loose.

That makes the engine harder to punish and more consistent from move to move.

## Mobility matters

Stonefish v4 also measures how many legal options remain available after a move.

All else being equal, it prefers positions where its pieces have more room to operate.

This is a major philosophical expansion. Stonefish is no longer valuing only immediate captures and short tactical sequences. It is beginning to value possibility itself in a more structured way: not random possibility, but useful future choice.

More mobility means more ways to improve, more tactical opportunities, and fewer positions where the engine traps itself.

## It understands repetition differently

Stonefish v4 introduces explicit repetition control.

When Stonefish is ahead by at least 2.5 points of material, it penalizes moves that revisit an already-seen position. In other words, when the engine has earned an advantage, it is less willing to drift back into a cycle and throw away conversion chances.

It also prefers less-repeated positions more generally and can favor pawn moves or captures when resetting the fifty-move clock becomes useful.

This gives v4 a stronger sense of direction. Getting ahead is not enough. The engine now tries to make progress with that advantage.

## A full positional stack

The positional layer goes far beyond one or two heuristics.

Stonefish v4 can use, in order, criteria including:

- strongest promotion,
- immediate castling,
- opening development,
- hanging-piece avoidance,
- repetition control while ahead,
- mobility,
- early queen discipline,
- repetition avoidance,
- fifty-move clock resets,
- endgame pawn progress,
- checking in the endgame,
- piece support,
- king freedom,
- center control,
- minor-piece centralization,
- king protection,
- pawn structure,
- rook activity,
- king placement,
- total board control.

These are not used to override v3’s tactical judgment. They are used lexicographically to break ties between moves that are already tactically equivalent.

That distinction is the core of Stonefish v4.

## The test: 2,000 direct games

For the v4 benchmark, we only care about games where Stonefish v4 itself played: 1,000 games against Stonefish v2 and 1,000 games against Stonefish v3.

Across those 2,000 direct games, Stonefish v4 scored:

**1,587 wins · 171 losses · 242 draws**

That is a **79.3% win rate** and an **8.6% loss rate**.

### Stonefish v4 vs Stonefish v2

Across 1,000 games:

- Stonefish v4: **977 wins, 1 loss, 22 draws**
- Stonefish v2: **1 win, 977 losses, 22 draws**
- Stonefish v4 win rate: **97.7%**

Against v2, the result is almost total domination.

### Stonefish v4 vs Stonefish v3

Across 1,000 games:

- Stonefish v4: **610 wins, 170 losses, 220 draws**
- Stonefish v3: **170 wins, 610 losses, 220 draws**
- Stonefish v4 win rate: **61.0%**

This is the more important comparison.

v4 and v3 share the same three-ply tactical core. v4’s advantage comes from what happens after that core has produced multiple equally strong candidates.

A 610–170 result against v3 shows that positional tie-breaking is not cosmetic. It changes outcomes at scale.

## Why v4 is better

Stonefish v4 is stronger because it adds quality without abandoning calculation.

It still sees the same immediate mates. It still avoids the same tactical disasters. It still uses three-ply worst-case material reasoning.

But now, when two moves are tactically tied, v4 can tell the difference between:

- development and stagnation,
- a safe piece and a hanging one,
- an active position and a cramped one,
- a productive king setup and an exposed one,
- a healthy pawn structure and a damaged one,
- useful repetition and pointless cycling,
- a rook with activity and a rook with none,
- control of the board and surrendering space.

That is why v4 represents a different kind of improvement from v3.

Stonefish v3 learned to calculate.

Stonefish v4 learns what a good position looks like.

## The revolution gets structure

Stonefish v1 was unpredictable.

Stonefish v2 became aware.

Stonefish v3 learned to calculate.

**Stonefish v4 learns to build.**

The engine no longer thinks only in terms of tactical survival and material sequences. It now develops pieces, protects its king, values activity, manages repetition, improves structure, and chooses positions with a future.

**Stonefish is no longer only finding good moves. It is learning how to create good positions.**
